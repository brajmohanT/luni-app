# Initial product assessment

Date: 2026-09-18. Status: discussion draft. No v1 scope decisions approved.

This assessment uses the founder's notes. It does not establish market demand, competitive differentiation, willingness to pay, or wellbeing outcomes. We need user evidence and market research to assess those claims.

## My reading of the intent

You want users to build an ongoing relationship with an AI that remembers them, matches their conversational preferences, and helps with daily life. You also want users to create separate personas for companionship, romance, and learning.

## Strong ideas to preserve

- Continuity: a user can return to an unfinished topic without repeating the background.
- Concrete care: remembering the flower preference and offering a requested reminder connects conversation with daily life.
- Selective memory: users need control over what the companion carries forward.
- Personality fit: users should be able to describe and adjust how they prefer to talk.

## Assumptions to challenge

- Do users want several personas, or one companion they know well? Persona creation adds setup and decisions before the first useful conversation.
- Does routing by mood help, or does the user feel misunderstood? Let users choose before we depend on inferred mood.
- Which memories should cross persona boundaries? Treat sharing as an explicit user choice; intimate conversations may contain information users do not want elsewhere.
- Does “I don't want to go away” mean satisfaction or dependence? Evaluate useful, voluntary return visits and whether users feel in control.
- What does “less restrictions” mean? Define the intended experience before promising it. Resolve the unclear handwritten sentence first.
- What does “impersonate someone” mean? Invented characters and copies of real people require different product decisions.

## Candidate directions to discuss

These are alternatives for prioritization, not approved scope.

1. Daily companion: talk through daily events, remember selected details, and resume past topics. Possible supporting feature: user-requested reminders.
2. Personal helper with a companion personality: capture commitments, remind users, and follow progress. Users would evaluate task reliability as well as conversation.
3. Persona-based companionship: create or choose a persona and develop a recurring conversation. Users would evaluate character consistency, customization, and relationship continuity.

My provisional preference is to explore the daily companion first. It connects your emotional-support and memory ideas while allowing us to test a small set of recurring scenarios. This is a scope judgment, not evidence that it will sell.

I propose deferring family hardware, shared households, broad device automation, automatic persona routing, and dedicated tutoring until we choose the primary experience. We still need to decide whether romance and multiple personas belong in that experience.

## Evidence to collect before committing to scope

- Interview a small first group, for example 5–8 target users, about their last relevant experience, current alternatives, and frustrations. Use this to discover patterns, not estimate market size.
- Walk through sample first-use and return-use conversations. Test whether users understand the promise and memory controls.
- Give users a short trial. Observe voluntary return visits, repeated scenarios, memory corrections, and reasons for leaving.
- Test a specific paid offer after users experience the benefit. Stated interest alone does not establish willingness to pay.
- Compare expected revenue with conversation, memory, voice if included, and operational costs.
